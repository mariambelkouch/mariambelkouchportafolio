01 — The thesis

Organizations are designed,
whether intentionally or not.

When organizations become more complex, the symptoms often surface as “People problems”: founders become bottlenecks, teams stop taking ownership, managers escalate decisions, and good people compensate for bad processes.

I look for the design problem underneath the symptom — then build the system that allows people to operate without compensating for it.

Complexity is inevitable.
Ambiguity is optional.

06 — About

Where this
actually comes from.

Fairness has a cost, and I've paid it.

At Hotel du Vin, tips had always gone to management. I moved them to the people working the shift — money leaving the hands of the people who'd approved it. I did it anyway.

Say the hard thing once, with dignity.

Moderating a job-search peer group, I had to tell someone — after real feedback, given more than once — that the group wasn't the right fit for her. She pushed back publicly. I answered once and let it go.

Discretion is part of the job, not a gap in the story.

The equity structure I designed at Havona is confidential at the mechanics level. I can describe the problem and how I approached it — not the specifics. That's not evasion. That's the job.

07 — The question I start with

I don't start by asking
what People problem needs fixing.

I start by asking what the organization is trying to accomplish, where the system is creating friction, and what needs to be redesigned so the people inside it can do their jobs without compensating for the design.
