01 — The thesis

Organizations are designed,
whether intentionally or not.

When organizations become more complex, the symptoms often surface as “People problems”: founders become bottlenecks, teams stop taking ownership, managers escalate decisions, and good people compensate for bad processes.

I look for the design problem underneath the symptom — then build the system that allows people to operate without compensating for it.

Complexity is inevitable.
Ambiguity is optional.

03 — Selected work

01

HAVONA · Decision architecture

From founder gravity
to distributed decisions.

The situation

Compensation decisions were being made case by case. Difficult conversations kept returning to leadership, and exceptions had nowhere else to land.

What I saw

The problem wasn't primarily that the founders couldn't delegate. Nobody else had sufficient standing to make the call.

The design

Built compensation bands, decision rights and documentation alongside equity and benefits architecture across 12 jurisdictions in EMEA, APAC and the US.

What changed

50% reduction in ad-hoc executive compensation decisions. Leadership could make decisions within a system rather than carrying every exception personally.

Delegation isn't giving someone a decision. It's giving them a system they can use to make that decision without you.
02

FIT 100 · Incentive architecture

When a “people problem”
was actually an incentive problem.

The situation

While partnering with Finance and looking at the numbers, I found that team members were receiving bonuses even when founder-set targets were not being met.

What I saw

The founders saw missed targets as a performance problem. But the system was teaching people that the reward remained available regardless of the outcome.

The design

Introduced regular 1:1s, reframed commercial accountability with the team, and introduced profit sharing so stronger performance created visible upside.

What changed

The bonus leakage was more than €60K. Targets were subsequently hit, and team members began bringing forward their own ideas for increasing revenue.

If you don't understand the economics, you can't fully understand the people problem.
03

HOTEL DU VIN · Work & team design

Designing the team
I wished I'd inherited.

The situation

The bar had not had a stable team for more than seven years. Turnover was high and agency staff filled the gaps.

What I saw

Hospitality had normalized giving managers the best shifts while junior team members absorbed the Friday nights, Saturday nights, Sundays and repeated closes.

The design

Put myself and my Assistant Manager into the rota. Everyone received one weekend off and one Friday off each month, with a balanced mix of daytime, closing and undesirable shifts — while still allowing individual preference.

What changed

7/7 retained for two years. 4/7 progressed into more senior roles. The team/rota model was subsequently adopted by other departments.

Fairness isn't a value statement. It's a scheduling decision.
